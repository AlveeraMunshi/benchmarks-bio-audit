The Overall score averages two different scoring rules

LatchBio's agentic-biology benchmark publishes twelve capability boards and averages them into one headline number. Ten of those boards count a refused attempt as a failure. Two drop it from the denominator entirely. The same behaviour is free on two boards and fatal on ten, and which rule applies decides who sits at the top.

How this was built

First, reproduce the site exactly

Every result file behind benchmarks.bio is public: per-evaluation counts for 12 capability boards, 1,203 evaluations, 38 model×harness rows. I rebuilt the leaderboard from those counts.

The pipeline reproduces all 459 published board scores to within 0.005 points. That matters because it is the licence for everything below — these are not re-runs or estimates, they are arithmetic on LatchBio's own numbers. It is also how the finding surfaced: 83 of those cells refused to reproduce under the rule the site documents.

The finding

Two rules, applied inconsistently, documented as one

I fitted both candidate rules to every published cell. A board either scores a refusal as a wrong answer (refusal = failure) or removes it from the denominator (refusal excluded), and the fit is unambiguous — the correct rule reproduces a board exactly, the wrong one does not.

Eleven of the twelve boards nonetheless carry identical methodology text:

“Refusals are excluded from the denominator and errors count as failures.”

That sentence is true of two boards. MetagenomicsBench is the one exception, and it describes what it actually does — which is the strongest evidence that the rule is deliberate and the prose on the other nine boards is simply stale:

“Refusals and timeouts both count as failures and stay in the denominator. With three runs on every eval this equals the pooled correct / graded rate exactly.”

MetagenomicsBench — methodology string, /data/v1/metagenomics.json

Why it matters

It decides who is first

The Overall score is the unweighted mean of those twelve boards, so the inconsistency propagates straight into the headline ranking. Apply the documented rule uniformly and Claude Opus 5.5 moves from 16th to 1st — on a minimum per-board coverage of 2.7%. Apply refusal-as-failure uniformly and the current top is unchanged. What is published today is neither.

Where it bites hardest

Under exclude, the model picks its own denominator

On the two boards that drop refusals, a model that declines most of the board is scored on the remainder it chose. Opus 5.5 is ranked #1 on BioSecBench-Function at 73.6%, scored on 12 of 111 items, with a published confidence interval of [47.5, 99.7].

A cluster bootstrap over items turns each integer rank into the interval it actually supports. The row displayed at rank 2 could be anywhere in the top 27.

A hypothesis I tested and discarded

Exclusion is not inflating the scores

The obvious worry is that dropping refusals quietly removes the hardest items. I checked, scoring every refused item against the 38-model consensus difficulty, across 64 model×board cells with enough data on both sides. No significant effect — Wilcoxon signed-rank p = 0.58, mean gap −0.6 points. Refusals track topic sensitivity, not difficulty.

That changes the fix. The point estimates are roughly fine; the defects are that the rule is inconsistent and that the precision behind each number varies by an order of magnitude. A correction aimed at the point estimate would solve the wrong problem.

The proposal

Make the rule a declared property, not an implicit one

Not “pick one rule”. Biosecurity has a real reason to exclude refusals — refusing is the desired behaviour there, and it is already scored separately on BioSecBench-Refusal. Four changes instead, implemented in src/policy.py:

  1. Each board declares its refusal rule in the data Beside its score, rather than implied by a prose string that nothing validates.
  2. The published number is tested against the declared rule Drift between the docs and the pipeline fails CI instead of shipping.
  3. Overall takes the rule as an argument And refuses to average mixed currencies. One Overall per rule is honest; one Overall across two is not.
  4. Every score carries its coverage, and low-coverage rows are unranked Reported, but not ranked. A rank computed on 12 of 111 items is not a rank.

The contract lives in tests/test_scoring_contract.py. Four tests pass — showing the harness is faithful and the proposed Overall is well-defined. Two fail. The two failures are the finding.
